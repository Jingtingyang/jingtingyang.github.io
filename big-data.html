Predicting Cross-Sectional Stock Returns

Comparing LASSO, Random Forest and XGBoost using firm characteristics, with an emphasis on out-of-sample performance and investment value.

PythonLASSORandom ForestXGBoostSHAP

01 / OVERVIEW

Research Overview

This research investigates whether machine learning models can predict the cross-section of US equity returns using firm characteristics. It compares predictive accuracy with the economic performance of long-short portfolios and tests robustness to transaction costs and market conditions.

02 / METHODOLOGY

Data & Methodology

Firm Characteristics

US equity firm characteristics from the Kelly dataset, evaluated across Core20, Core40, Core60, Core80 and Core100 feature sets.

Model Comparison

LASSO, Random Forest and XGBoost are compared using a time-ordered training, validation and out-of-sample testing framework.

Portfolio Evaluation

Equal-weighted 10–1 long-short portfolios are rebalanced monthly and assessed using Sharpe ratio, volatility, drawdown, turnover and transaction costs.

Key finding

Core20 XGBoost achieved an out-of-sample annualised return of approximately 50.65%, a Sharpe ratio of 1.85 and maximum drawdown of approximately −35.58%. Its Sharpe ratio decreased to approximately 1.65 at 50 bps transaction costs.

Research results are historical, based on the study's specified methodology, and do not imply future investment performance.
